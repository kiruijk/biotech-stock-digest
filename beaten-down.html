The Theses

Fate Therapeutics (FATE)

-98% from its high · +148% off its 52-week low · cash 54% of market cap

Why it fell

Fate was a 2021 cell-therapy darling at over $100 a share. In January 2023 Janssen ended their collaboration, and Fate cut more than half its workforce and dropped most of its cancer programs. Off-the-shelf NK-cell therapies struggled to show durable responses, and the stock lost about 98% from its peak.

Why it could recover

Fate has moved into autoimmune disease with FT819, an off-the-shelf CAR T cell made from induced pluripotent stem cells. It needs no collection of the patient's own cells and is given as a single dose. Phase 1 data in 16 lupus patients showed no dose-limiting toxicities, no severe cytokine release and no neurotoxicity, plus improvements in disease activity and kidney protein levels. The first patient in RECLAIM-LN, a potentially registrational Phase 2 in lupus nephritis, has been dosed as an outpatient. Cash of $154M funds operations into 2028, over half the ~$280M market cap.

What would break the thesis

Responses that fade without the deep B-cell reset that autologous CAR T achieves, or safety problems as more patients are treated. Faster progress from competitors, such as in-vivo CAR T or T-cell engagers in lupus, would also hurt. Another large equity raise before RECLAIM-LN reads out would dilute holders heavily.

Altimmune (ALT)

-98% from its high · +15% off its 52-week low · cash 90% of market cap

Why it fell

Altimmune's share price has fallen for years through failed vaccine and hepatitis programs, dilution and a reverse split. More recently, the June 2025 IMPACT Phase 2b MASH readout of pemvidutide hit MASH resolution strongly (59% vs 19% on placebo). However, fibrosis improvement at 24 weeks was not statistically significant, and investors sold off hard. Fibrosis is the endpoint that matters most for approval.

Why it could recover

The FDA later granted pemvidutide Breakthrough Therapy designation, and the Phase 3 PERFORMA MASH trial started in August 2026. Positive Phase 2 data in alcohol use disorder, plus a fully enrolled trial in alcohol-associated liver disease, add upside in markets with few drugs. The balance sheet is the key point: about $518M in cash against a ~$575M market cap, so investors pay under $60M for a Phase 3 asset with Breakthrough designation.

What would break the thesis

Pivotal MASH data are not due until 2029, which leaves a long wait in which competitors keep advancing. These include Rezdiffra, Wegovy for MASH and pharma-owned FGF21 drugs. Weak 48-week fibrosis data or a failed alcohol-related readout would remove the near-term support. Phase 3 costs could also force another raise before 2029.

Zentalis Pharmaceuticals (ZNTL)

-97% from its high · +112% off its 52-week low · cash 68% of market cap

Why it fell

Zentalis peaked at about $85 in November 2021 with a broad oncology pipeline. In June 2024 the FDA placed partial clinical holds on three azenosertib trials after two patient deaths from presumed sepsis, and the stock fell more than 45% in a day. The hold was lifted later that year, but the company then narrowed to a single drug and a single indication.

Why it could recover

Azenosertib, a WEE1 inhibitor, now targets Cyclin E1-positive platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, roughly half of a population with poor options. The drug posted a 31% response rate in that group. The FDA has aligned on the dose and patient population for a potential accelerated approval, and registration-intent topline data from DENALI Part 2 are due in the first half of 2027. With $175M of cash at June 30 plus a $92.6M raise in August 2026, the company is funded through the readout. Its ~$260M market cap is small for a drug with a near-term approval path.

What would break the thesis

A DENALI Part 2 response rate well below the earlier ~31%, short response durations, or a return of serious infections or deaths. Any FDA change on the accelerated-approval path would hurt, as would a slow launch in a niche indication. With one drug, a miss leaves little else to support the stock.

Recursion Pharmaceuticals (RXRX)

-90% from its high · +42% off its 52-week low · cash 25% of market cap

Why it fell

Recursion listed in 2021 as the flagship AI drug-discovery company and peaked above $40. Early clinical programs disappointed: in May 2025, after merging with Exscientia, it cut or paused several including REC-994 in cerebral cavernous malformation and REC-2282 in NF2 meningioma. It burns cash heavily (about $375M planned for 2026), and partnership revenue has fallen.

Why it could recover

The pipeline is now narrower and mostly in cancer, with the first real tests coming. Phase 2 data for REC-4881 in familial adenomatous polyposis are due in November 2026, alongside an FDA update on a registrational path. Partnerships with Roche/Genentech, Sanofi and Bayer bring milestone payments. A restructured Tempus deal in September 2026 cut Recursion's data costs and added a $12M license for its RNA model. Cash of $557M lasts into early 2028. For ~$2.1B, investors get one of the largest biological datasets in the industry, and a single clinical win for an AI-designed drug would change how the market values it.

What would break the thesis

Weak REC-4881 data or an unclear path to approval. Continued pipeline cuts would suggest the platform isn't producing better drugs. If burn stays above $30M a month without clinical wins, another dilutive raise before 2028 becomes likely.

ADMA Biologics (ADMA)

-59% from its high · +31% off its 52-week low · cash 6% of market cap

Why it fell

ADMA is not a clinical-stage story. It is a profitable maker of plasma-derived immunoglobulins that peaked in April 2025 at about $24. In March 2026 short seller Culper Research alleged channel stuffing through a related-party distributor. In May 2026 ADMA cut its 2026 revenue guidance from more than $635M to $530–560M and withdrew its long-term targets, citing immunoglobulin pricing pressure, competition and excess channel inventory. The stock fell over 24% after hours, and securities class actions followed.

Why it could recover

The core business held up in Q2 2026. Revenue of its premium product ASCENIV grew 24%, gross margin rose to 69% (from 55%) on its yield-enhanced manufacturing process, and GAAP net income was $37.8M. The company reiterated guidance of $170–200M adjusted net income for 2026, which puts the ~$2.3B market cap at roughly 11–13x earnings, cheap for a growing, cash-generating biologics maker. A director bought shares in May 2026.

What would break the thesis

Evidence supporting the channel-stuffing claims, or another guidance cut as inventory clears. Faster price erosion across the immunoglobulin market, or losses in the class actions, would also hurt. BIVIGAM revenue fell 54% year over year, and ADMA needs ASCENIV growth to keep offsetting that.

List last reviewed Sep 29, 2026. Opinion, not a recommendation: these stocks are high-risk and many clinical-stage companies never recover.
