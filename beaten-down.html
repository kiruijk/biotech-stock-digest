The Theses

enGene Therapeutics (ENGN)

-84% from its 18-month high · +38% off its 52-week low · net cash 182% of market cap

Why it fell

enGene peaked near $12 in January 2026 ahead of pivotal data for detalimogene, a non-viral gene therapy delivered directly into the bladder for BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer. The May 7, 2026 update showed a 54% complete response rate at any time and 43% at six months, but durability disappointed: the estimated 12-month complete response rate was 25%, with a median response duration of 37 weeks. The stock fell about 76%.

Why it could recover

The market now values enGene below its cash: about $266M in cash and investments (July 31, 2026) with about $25M of debt, against a ~$130M market cap. The drug still works in a meaningful share of patients, rarely lets disease progress to muscle-invasive cancer (3.2%), and is easy to give without special handling. A BLA submission is planned to begin in Q4 2026 after a pre-BLA meeting, so a first approval could come within about 18 months. The interim chief medical officer bought shares at $1.97 in September 2026.

What would break the thesis

The FDA treating a 25% 12-month response rate as too weak for approval, or approving it with a narrow label. Even if approved, the market is crowded with more durable options: J&J's TAR-200 (Inlexzo), Ferring's Adstiladrin and CG Oncology's cretostimogene. Management spending the cash on a hard-to-win launch, not preserving value, would also break the case.

Wave Life Sciences (WVE)

-82% from its 18-month high · +7% off its 52-week low · net cash 64% of market cap

Why it fell

Wave ran up to about $21 in December 2025 as investors bet on WVE-007, its RNA-based obesity drug. On March 26, 2026, higher-dose data disappointed: the 240 mg dose produced only about 0.9% placebo-adjusted weight loss at six months, even with a ~14% cut in visceral fat. The stock fell about 50% in a day and kept sliding as obesity hopes faded, with revenue down as collaboration payments dropped.

Why it could recover

The obesity program was hiding the more important asset. WVE-006 achieved the first therapeutic RNA editing in humans. In alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency it cuts harmful Z-AAT and restores protective M-AAT to levels like those of healthy carriers, with subcutaneous dosing and good safety. Updated September 2026 data were positive. GSK handed back full rights, and Wave plans to seek accelerated approval, which would make it the first RNA-editing drug with a regulatory path. $491M in cash (no debt) funds operations into Q3 2028, so the market (about $720M) values the entire RNA platform at about $230M.

What would break the thesis

The FDA declining accelerated approval based on protein levels and requiring a long lung-function outcomes trial. Durability or safety problems with repeat dosing of WVE-006. Competing alpha-1 approaches (RNA editing from Korro and Beam's base editing) moving faster. Spending heavily to keep WVE-007 alive despite weak weight-loss data would also weigh on the stock.

Olema Pharmaceuticals (OLMA)

-77% from its 18-month high · +6% off its 52-week low · net cash 63% of market cap

Why it fell

Olema peaked near $36 in December 2025 on enthusiasm for palazestrant, its oral estrogen-receptor drug for breast cancer. In August 2026, top-line data from the pivotal OPERA-01 trial slipped from 2026 to the first quarter of 2027. In September, AstraZeneca's SERENA-4 trial of camizestrant (a first-line combination) missed its primary endpoint, casting doubt on the whole oral SERD class. Olema fell 15% that day, and Stifel cut its target.

Why it could recover

Nothing Olema owns has failed. OPERA-01 is fully enrolled and tests palazestrant alone in second- and third-line ER+/HER2- breast cancer, a different setting from SERENA-4, and oral SERDs have already won approval in later-line settings. A second Phase 3, OPERA-02, is testing it with a CDK4/6 inhibitor in first-line patients. The company has $461M in cash and almost no debt, with a pivotal readout months away, so about 60% of its ~$750M value is cash.

What would break the thesis

OPERA-01 missing its primary endpoint in Q1 2027, or winning by too small a margin to compete with approved oral SERDs such as Lilly's imlunestrant and Menarini's elacestrant. Further class-wide setbacks that shrink the market, or a large raise before the readout, would also hurt.

Lyell Immunopharma (LYEL)

-76% from its 18-month high · 0% off its 52-week low · net cash 99% of market cap

Why it fell

Lyell spiked to about $45 in December 2025 after a milestone in its pivotal PiNACLE trial and a round of analyst upgrades, then gave back about three-quarters of that. No trial failed. The fall reflects the spike unwinding, a cash balance that dropped from $384M at the end of 2024 to $228M by June 2026, and investor fatigue with autologous CAR T, which requires making each dose from the patient's own cells.

Why it could recover

Ronde-cel is a dual-targeting CD19/CD20 CAR T for large B-cell lymphoma. Targeting two proteins is designed to reduce relapses in which the cancer stops expressing CD19. Data in more than 100 patients at EHA 2026 showed no grade 3 or higher cytokine release syndrome and low rates of severe neurotoxicity, which supports outpatient treatment and could make it easier for community centers to adopt. PiNACLE reports more data in the second half of 2026 and pivotal data in mid-2027, with a BLA planned for the second half of 2027. A head-to-head Phase 3 against approved CAR Ts in second-line patients is also running. The market values Lyell at about its cash ($228M against a ~$235M market cap), so a drug with pivotal data due in 2027 is priced close to zero.

What would break the thesis

Cash lasts only into Q3 2027, before the BLA, so a sizable raise is likely and could be dilutive if the stock is still near its lows. Third-line-plus lymphoma is crowded: Yescarta and Breyanzi are established, and off-the-shelf bispecifics like glofitamab and epcoritamab are taking share from CAR T. Pivotal response or durability data that aren't clearly better than approved CAR Ts, or any new safety signal, would leave little reason to own it.

Altimmune (ALT)

-64% from its 18-month high · +7% off its 52-week low · net cash 90% of market cap

Why it fell

Altimmune's share price has fallen for years through failed vaccine and hepatitis programs, dilution and a reverse split. More recently, the June 2025 IMPACT Phase 2b MASH readout of pemvidutide hit MASH resolution strongly (59% vs 19% on placebo). However, fibrosis improvement at 24 weeks was not statistically significant, and investors sold off hard. Fibrosis is the endpoint that matters most for approval.

Why it could recover

The FDA later granted pemvidutide Breakthrough Therapy designation, and the Phase 3 PERFORMA MASH trial started in August 2026. Positive Phase 2 data in alcohol use disorder, plus a fully enrolled trial in alcohol-associated liver disease, add upside in markets with few drugs. The balance sheet is the key point: about $518M in cash against a ~$575M market cap, so investors pay under $60M for a Phase 3 asset with Breakthrough designation.

What would break the thesis

Pivotal MASH data are not due until 2029, which leaves a long wait in which competitors keep advancing. These include Rezdiffra, Wegovy for MASH and pharma-owned FGF21 drugs. Weak 48-week fibrosis data or a failed alcohol-related readout would remove the near-term support. Phase 3 costs could also force another raise before 2029.

Zentalis Pharmaceuticals (ZNTL)

-61% from its 18-month high · +103% off its 52-week low · net cash 71% of market cap

Why it fell

Zentalis peaked at about $85 in November 2021 with a broad oncology pipeline. In June 2024 the FDA placed partial clinical holds on three azenosertib trials after two patient deaths from presumed sepsis, and the stock fell more than 45% in a day. The hold was lifted later that year, but the company then narrowed to a single drug and a single indication.

Why it could recover

Azenosertib, a WEE1 inhibitor, now targets Cyclin E1-positive platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, roughly half of a population with poor options. The drug posted a 31% response rate in that group. The FDA has aligned on the dose and patient population for a potential accelerated approval, and registration-intent topline data from DENALI Part 2 are due in the first half of 2027. With $175M of cash at June 30 plus a $92.6M raise in August 2026, the company is funded through the readout. Its ~$260M market cap is small for a drug with a near-term approval path.

What would break the thesis

A DENALI Part 2 response rate well below the earlier ~31%, short response durations, or a return of serious infections or deaths. Any FDA change on the accelerated-approval path would hurt, as would a slow launch in a niche indication. With one drug, a miss leaves little else to support the stock.

ADMA Biologics (ADMA)

-59% from its 18-month high · +31% off its 52-week low · net cash -3% of market cap

Why it fell

ADMA is not a clinical-stage story. It is a profitable maker of plasma-derived immunoglobulins that peaked in April 2025 at about $24. In March 2026 short seller Culper Research alleged channel stuffing through a related-party distributor. In May 2026 ADMA cut its 2026 revenue guidance from more than $635M to $530–560M and withdrew its long-term targets, citing immunoglobulin pricing pressure, competition and excess channel inventory. The stock fell over 24% after hours, and securities class actions followed.

Why it could recover

The core business held up in Q2 2026. Revenue of its premium product ASCENIV grew 24%, gross margin rose to 69% (from 55%) on its yield-enhanced manufacturing process, and GAAP net income was $37.8M. The company reiterated guidance of $170–200M adjusted net income for 2026, which puts the ~$2.3B market cap at roughly 11–13x earnings, cheap for a growing, cash-generating biologics maker. A director bought shares in May 2026.

What would break the thesis

Evidence supporting the channel-stuffing claims, or another guidance cut as inventory clears. Faster price erosion across the immunoglobulin market, or losses in the class actions, would also hurt. BIVIGAM revenue fell 54% year over year, and ADMA needs ASCENIV growth to keep offsetting that.

Recursion Pharmaceuticals (RXRX)

-40% from its 18-month high · +43% off its 52-week low · net cash 24% of market cap

Why it fell

Recursion listed in 2021 as the flagship AI drug-discovery company and peaked above $40. Early clinical programs disappointed: in May 2025, after merging with Exscientia, it cut or paused several including REC-994 in cerebral cavernous malformation and REC-2282 in NF2 meningioma. It burns cash heavily (about $375M planned for 2026), and partnership revenue has fallen.

Why it could recover

The pipeline is now narrower and mostly in cancer, with the first real tests coming. Phase 2 data for REC-4881 in familial adenomatous polyposis are due in November 2026, alongside an FDA update on a registrational path. Partnerships with Roche/Genentech, Sanofi and Bayer bring milestone payments. A restructured Tempus deal in September 2026 cut Recursion's data costs and added a $12M license for its RNA model. Cash of $557M lasts into early 2028. For ~$2.1B, investors get one of the largest biological datasets in the industry, and a single clinical win for an AI-designed drug would change how the market values it.

What would break the thesis

Weak REC-4881 data or an unclear path to approval. Continued pipeline cuts would suggest the platform isn't producing better drugs. If burn stays above $30M a month without clinical wins, another dilutive raise before 2028 becomes likely.
