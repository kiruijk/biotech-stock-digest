Viking Therapeutics (VKTX)

Obesity & Metabolic

Company Overview

Viking Therapeutics (VKTX) is a San Diego-based clinical-stage company focused on metabolic and endocrine diseases, led by co-founder and CEO Brian Lian.

Its lead program, VK2735, is a dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist, the same mechanism as Eli Lilly's Mounjaro/Zepbound, developed in both injectable and oral forms. The subcutaneous Phase 3 VANQUISH program is fully enrolled: VANQUISH-1 (~4,500 adults with obesity) and VANQUISH-2 (~1,000 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes), each dosing weekly for 78 weeks. The oral tablet produced up to 12.2% weight loss after 13 weeks in the Phase 2 VENTURE-Oral study, and Viking expects to start oral Phase 3 in Q4 2026. On September 22, 2026, Viking reported maintenance-dosing results: after 21 weeks of weekly dosing (16–19% weight loss), patients switched to every-other-week dosing kept up to 97% of their weight loss, and those switched to monthly dosing up to 90%, versus 61% for patients switched to placebo. An exploratory cohort that stayed on weekly dosing for 33 weeks reached 22% placebo-adjusted weight loss with no plateau.

Earlier programs include VK3019, an amylin agonist that entered Phase 1 in Q2 2026, and VK2809, a thyroid hormone receptor-beta agonist studied in MASH.

Investor thesis: VKTX is one of the few independent companies with both an injectable and an oral GLP-1/GIP program in late-stage development, which keeps it at the center of obesity M&A speculation. Shares rose more than 27% on the maintenance data, and the next day Viking announced a $200M stock offering and $200M of convertible notes due 2032 to fund VK2735 and VK3019. The risks are spending, dilution and scale: Phase 3 costs pushed the first-half 2026 net loss to $286M, and competing with Lilly and Novo alone would require major manufacturing and commercial investment.
