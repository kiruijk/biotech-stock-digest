Structure Therapeutics (GPCR)

Obesity & Metabolic

Company Overview

Structure Therapeutics (GPCR) is a South San Francisco-based clinical-stage company founded by CEO Raymond Stevens that uses structure-based drug discovery to design oral small molecules against G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) — the receptor family behind GLP-1 and most metabolic hormones.

Its lead program, aleniglipron, is a once-daily oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist for obesity. Unlike peptides, small molecules are cheaper to manufacture at scale and have no food/water dosing restrictions. The first patients have been dosed in the Phase 3 ACCOMPLISH program, while the ACCESS open-label extension and several supplementary trials continue to generate data.

Investor thesis: GPCR is one of the few independent companies with a late-stage oral small-molecule GLP-1, a format big pharma is racing to own. With $1.3B in cash funding operations through the end of 2028 and a newly hired Chief Commercial Officer, the company is positioning to compete directly with Lilly's orforglipron. Phase 2 ACCESS II showed up to 16.3% weight loss, yet the stock is down roughly half in 2026 as investors question whether an independent company can win share against Lilly and Novo.
