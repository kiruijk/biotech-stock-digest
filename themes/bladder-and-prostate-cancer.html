Bladder & Prostate Cancer

8 companies tracked

Genitourinary cancers are large markets that are changing fast. In early bladder cancer, a wave of therapies delivered directly into the bladder aims to spare patients bladder removal and repeat surgeries. In prostate cancer, PSMA-targeted drugs and T cell engagers are moving from late-stage disease toward earlier treatment.

The Science

Bladder cancer basics

About three-quarters of new bladder cancers are non-muscle-invasive (NMIBC): confined to the bladder lining. Standard care is surgical removal through the urethra (TURBT), followed for higher-risk disease by BCG, a weakened bacterium instilled into the bladder to provoke an immune response. When BCG stops working (BCG-unresponsive), the traditional option is removing the bladder (cystectomy), a major operation that many patients want to avoid. Lower-risk tumors often come back again and again, meaning repeated surgeries.

New bladder therapies

Most new treatments are intravesical, placed directly into the bladder through a catheter. They include gene therapies, oncolytic viruses, an IL-15 immune stimulant and slow-release drug devices and gels. In BCG-unresponsive disease, regulators accept single-arm trials measuring the complete response rate, so how long responses last (for example at 12 months) is the key way to compare products.

Prostate cancer

Localized prostate cancer is treated with surgery or radiation, often combined with hormone therapy, and new drugs aim to lower the chance of recurrence. Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) keeps growing despite hormone therapy. Many tumors display PSMA, the target of radioligand therapy (Pluvicto) and of experimental T cell engagers that link T cells to cancer cells. Masked engagers switch on only inside tumors, to reduce side effects.

What investors watch

  • Complete response rate and 12-month durability in bladder cancer.
  • Ease of use in urology clinics: storage, handling and treatment schedule.
  • Disease-free survival in localized prostate cancer, and PSA and imaging responses in mCRPC.
  • Crowding: several approved and late-stage options now compete for the same patients.
