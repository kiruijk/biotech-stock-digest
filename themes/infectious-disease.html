Infectious Disease

5 companies tracked

Chronic viral infections such as hepatitis delta, hepatitis B and HIV remain large unmet needs. New antibodies and RNA-silencing drugs aim for deeper viral suppression or even functional cures, often using combinations that attack the virus from more than one angle.

The Science

Hepatitis delta

Hepatitis delta virus (HDV) is an incomplete virus that can only infect people who already have hepatitis B, because it borrows hepatitis B's surface protein (HBsAg) to build its outer coat. WHO estimates it affects about 5% of people with chronic hepatitis B, and it is the most severe form of viral hepatitis, often progressing to cirrhosis within years. Treatment options are limited: the entry inhibitor bulevirtide is approved in Europe.

How new therapies work

  • Neutralizing antibodies bind the viral surface protein, blocking infection of new liver cells and helping the immune system clear infected ones.
  • siRNA drugs silence viral RNA inside liver cells, reducing production of the surface protein that both hepatitis B and delta depend on.
  • Combinations of the two aim to suppress the virus more deeply than either alone.

What investors watch

  • Undetectable virus levels (HDV RNA) and normalized liver enzymes (ALT), often combined into a single response endpoint.
  • Durability after stopping treatment: whether patients can reach a functional cure rather than lifelong therapy.
  • Diagnosis rates: many infected people are never tested for hepatitis delta, which affects the real size of the market.
  • Platform spillover: antibody and T cell-engager platforms built for infectious disease are increasingly being applied to cancer.
